## Purpose and context

This memo sets out the strategy team's recommendations for the FY27 Gen Z plan. It draws on the May customer survey, the Q2 results reviewed in July, the near final data from the spring personalization pilot and the latest market and channel research. The board's ambition is to raise the Gen Z share of revenue to 30% by the end of FY28, and FY27 is the year in which the foundations for that goal must be set.

The recommendations are intentionally focused. We are proposing three priorities and one disciplined test, rather than a long list of initiatives, because the evidence this year has pointed consistently at a small number of problems that customers care about.

## What we learned

### The pilot points toward caution

The personalization pilot was the most important experiment of the year and its results are the least comfortable. Shoppers who bought personalized products came back within 90 days at a lower rate than the matched control group, 18% against 24%. Only a small minority chose the paid premium tier for personalized fit, and most took the free monogram instead. Lead time of about 12 days was the most common reason customers gave for not ordering again. Lower returns on personalized items are a genuine positive, but they do not offset weaker repeat behaviour and the extra operational cost. The early enthusiasm seen in the first weeks proved to be a poor guide to the final outcome.

Stated willingness to pay in the external study was much higher than revealed behaviour in the pilot, which is a familiar gap in consumer research but a costly one to ignore when sizing a national program.

### Customers want the basics right

The May survey showed that fit inconsistency is the leading frustration for Gen Z customers, followed by price versus value, sizing across categories and knit upper durability. Personalization was named by a small share of respondents as their top frustration. Gen Z NPS of 22 compares with 49 among customers aged 28 and older. Across all customers, Northstar NPS held steady at 31 in Q2. The gap between young and older customers is where the opportunity lies.

Support contacts through the third quarter tell the same story. Sizing and fit questions are the largest theme among younger customers, Knit Runner 2 wear complaints are rising, and customers who bought personalized items contacted us about delivery far more often than average.

### Spending is shifting to Social Shop

Social Shop grew faster than any other channel in Q2 and Gen Z conversion there continues to outperform millennials. External research supports the direction of travel: the 2026 trends report finds that just over a quarter of Gen Z athletic footwear purchases now route through social commerce, and the channel shift note estimates that marketplaces and social platforms together captured about a third of Gen Z athletic spend this year, ahead of brand owned sites. Customers also say they trust peer reviews and creator videos more than brand advertising. Our spend mix has not kept pace.

## Recommendations

We recommend that the leadership team approve the following for the FY27 plan.

- Build the plan around fit. We recommend launching a cross category Fit Promise, a free exchange guarantee covering every apparel and footwear size, in Q1 FY27.
- Fix durability before scaling the running range. Complete the revised knit construction for the Knit Runner 2 and extend wear testing to all knit uppers.
- Shift marketing toward community. Prioritize creator partnerships, run club and studio programs and Social Shop, with measurement of repeat purchase and not only reach.
- Treat personalization as an experiment. Personalization should stay a limited test in FY27 rather than a national launch until repeat purchase beats the control group.

On the fit commitment, the guarantee would be supported by a unified grading standard so that the promise is credible and the cost of exchanges is contained. We expect the largest benefit among Studio Social customers, who are the highest value segment and the most frustrated by inconsistency.

On personalization, the continuing test should be narrow and cheap. We suggest retaining the monogram option, which customers took readily at no extra charge, keeping the in store scan in the current three locations, and exploring whether faster production can bring lead time under a week. We would revisit expansion only if a repeat of the measurement shows repeat purchase ahead of control.

## Budget

We propose a FY27 Gen Z marketing budget of $24 million, of which 40% would shift to creator and community programs. The proposed allocation is set out below.

- Creator and community programs: $9.6 million
- Social Shop and performance channels: $5.4 million
- Fit Promise launch and fit education content: $4.5 million
- Brand and awareness building: $3.0 million
- Measurement and test reserve, including the personalization test: $1.5 million

The total is within the planning envelope provided by finance. The shift toward creators and community is funded mainly by reducing broad reach advertising and by trimming spend on promotions that discount core styles.

## Risks

- An exchange guarantee could increase returns volume if customers order multiple sizes. We will cap exposure and monitor by category.
- Creator programs could miss on authenticity. We will limit the number of partners and measure incremental sales.
- A narrow personalization test may leave Northstar behind if competitors solve lead time first. The monitoring plan includes quarterly competitor checks.
- Revised knit construction may slip, delaying the running range push. A fallback is to promote other styles in the interim.

## Next steps

If leadership agrees in principle, the strategy team will work with product, marketing, finance and customer support over October to finalize budgets and the Fit Promise design. The detailed FY27 plan comes to the leadership meeting in November, followed by board review. We would also like to agree on one definition of Gen Z for target tracking so that the 30% goal is measured consistently, and ask insights to refresh the survey in spring to track change in the main pain points.
