# Retail Channel Shift Note

Where Gen Z athletic spend is moving in 2026

Cobalt Insight Partners - Retail Practice

July 2026

Public note

Fictional data created for the MarketSignal demo.

## Summary

Gen Z athletic spend is migrating across channels faster than most brand plans assume. Discovery, comparison and purchase are no longer tied to one place, and a shopper may watch a creator video in the morning, read reviews on a marketplace at lunch and complete the order wherever the delivery promise looks best. This note sets out the main movements this year and what they imply for brands.

- Marketplace and social commerce together took 34% of Gen Z athletic spend in 2026, compared with 22% for brand owned sites.
- Brand owned sites remain important for loyal customers but are losing share of first purchases.
- Specialty running stores are weakening, while store in store programs are widening reach for value brands.
- Delivery expectations are tightening, and the channels that meet them are gaining share.

## Marketplaces and social commerce

Marketplaces win on convenience, price comparison and a deep base of reviews. Social commerce wins on discovery, since a creator video shows the product on a body, and comment threads act as an informal size guide. The two routes complement one another, and many Gen Z shoppers use one to discover and the other to buy.

- Marketplace listings are used as a price and review check even by shoppers who buy elsewhere.
- Social shopping converts best when the video, the product page and the reply to a comment all tell the same story.
- Brands with thin review histories or inconsistent sizing guidance are penalized in both routes.

## Brand sites

Brand sites still carry the highest margin and the richest customer data, and they remain the preferred route for repeat buyers and for products with a story, such as limited drops. But they are less often the first stop. Shoppers arrive with a shortlist already formed and use the brand site to confirm details, check availability and look for a reason to trust the seller.

- Conversion is strongest where size guidance, returns terms and delivery dates are visible without scrolling.
- Loyalty programs and apps help brand sites hold on to existing customers but do little to attract new ones.
- Brands that mirror their best social content on the site see a stronger link between the two.

## Specialty running stores

Specialty running stores are losing Gen Z share fastest, falling to 9% of their athletic footwear purchases. Younger runners still value gait analysis and expert fitting, but they are more likely to receive advice from a club, a coach or a group chat, and to buy online once they know what they want. Store traffic from the cohort is skewed to occasional visits for fitting and race preparation, rather than regular replenishment.

- Fitting services remain a differentiator, though shoppers want them linked to a clear exchange promise.
- Independent stores that run community events and group runs keep more of their younger customers.

## Store in store programs

Shelf space inside large general retailers has become a quiet growth route for value oriented brands. Reaching shoppers inside a store they already visit regularly lowers acquisition costs and gives the brand a physical presence in towns where it would not open its own location. One value athleisure label has expanded its footprint here to more than a thousand doors in a single year. Regular product drops give shoppers a reason to return to the shelf.

- Store in store works best for simple, repeatable items with low fit risk, such as leggings and tops.
- It is less suited to products that need fitting or to personalization, since staff and inventory are limited.

## Delivery expectations

Faster delivery has become a basic requirement for much of the cohort, particularly for everyday items. Shoppers use promised delivery dates as a deciding factor between otherwise similar listings, and a missed date can end a relationship. The marketplaces and larger platforms have set the standard, and brand sites and smaller stores are being measured against it.

- Show the delivery date before checkout, and keep to it.
- Offer store pickup where inventory allows, and make local stock visible online.
- Treat made to order products as a special case with clearly stated lead times.

## What it means for brands

- Plan for a multi channel journey, not a single funnel. Measure the contribution of each route to discovery, consideration and purchase.
- Invest in the quality of product content, reviews and sizing information, which travel with the product across channels.
- Use marketplaces and social routes to reach new buyers, and the brand site and app to convert them into loyal customers.
- Re-examine store footprints and partnerships so that they support fitting, community and fast fulfilment.
- Align delivery promises with what each channel can actually keep.
